Colossians 1:15–20

ThePreeminenceof Christ

“that in everything he might be preeminent.” Colossians 1:18, ESV

Six claims stand behind that word everything. Each has a color here. Follow any of them through the rest of Scripture.

The passage

Six verses. The ESV sets them under the heading “The Preeminence of Christ.” Read them four ways.

Six claims, one color each. Tap any phrase.

15He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.

16For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things were created through him and for him.

17And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together.

18And he is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent.

19For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell,

20and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross.

Each color marks one claim about the Son. The bold line in verse 18 is the point of all six.

The ESV says all seven times and everything once. Greek uses one word for all eight. Paul leaves nothing outside the Son’s rule: no realm, no power, no moment.

Creation comes by him, through him, and for him. Reconciliation comes through him and to himself. In verse 16, by him can also be read in him (ESV footnote).

Four statements in the present tense. They say who he is before they say what he has done.

Six claims

Open a claim for its cross references, a key word, and a voice from the commentaries.

    Two realms

    The passage makes one movement twice: once for creation, once for the new creation that begins at the empty tomb. Slide between them. The frame holds while the words change.

    I II III IV V VI
    • He is “He is the image of the invisible God” v. 15“He is the beginning” v. 18
    • The firstborn “the firstborn of all creation” v. 15“the firstborn from the dead” v. 18
    • For in him “For by him all things were created” v. 16“For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell” v. 19
    • Through him, to him “all things were created through him and for him” v. 16“through him to reconcile to himself all things” v. 20
    • Heaven and earth “in heaven and on earth” v. 16“whether on earth or in heaven” v. 20

    Between the two movements stands the purpose of both“that in everything he might be preeminent.”

    The Greek is tighter still. By him (v. 16) and in him (v. 19) translate the same two words. So do for him (v. 16) and to himself (v. 20).

    Firstborn

    Verse 15 calls the Son “the firstborn of all creation.” Everything depends on what kind of first that is.

    Heir and ruler. He stands over all creation and is not counted among the things made.

    Then he is one of the things made, only the earliest. Arius taught this in the fourth century, and Jehovah’s Witnesses teach it now. The passage rules it out.

    Three reasons the passage means rank

    1. The next sentence

      “For by him all things were created” v. 16

      Whoever made all things cannot be one of them.

    2. The royal title

      “And I will make him the firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth.” Psalm 89:27

      David was the youngest of Jesse’s sons. God gave him the first place.

    3. The nation

      “Israel is my firstborn son” Exodus 4:22

      Israel was not the oldest nation. It was the one God set first.

    The NIV, CSB, and NKJV make the sense plain in translation: “the firstborn over all creation.”

    The witnesses

    Five other chapters make these claims. Colossians 1 is the only one that makes all six. Tap a mark to read it.

    A blank means the chapter does not state the claim outright. It does not mean the writer denied it.

    In everything

    The word behind the title.

    prōteuōn

    πρωτεύων: to be first, to hold first place

    The verb appears once in the New Testament, here. Its nearest relative describes Diotrephes, “who likes to put himself first” (3 John 9). The King James Version gives both men the same word, preeminence. Only one of them has a right to it.

    • ESV“that in everything he might be preeminent”
    • KJV“that in all things he might have the preeminence”
    • NIV“so that in everything he might have the supremacy”
    • CSB“so that he might come to have first place in everything”
